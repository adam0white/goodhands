## What playing felt like

**Best moment:** Sevda’s first-day call. A grandmother wants extra housework, a parent prioritises childcare, and the worker has a bus and a dependent mother. “This week only. Please tell them that” made my compromise feel like an obligation to someone. Pınar’s first explanation of her previous job also gave me a person to care about.

**Worst moment:** The Arslans asking whether I had found anyone immediately after I had placed Sevda and received their deposit. I wanted a truthful answer and could not see one. I doubted whether my placement had registered until Families showed “filled.”

**Boredom and quitting:** Boredom became explicit around 04:45. I waited because I wanted consequences from the unchecked promise. After the call, I did not want another wait or another round of interview administration. I quit at 06:18. The office-closed summary arrived at the end; I did not test what continuing from it would do.

**Optional question, after the first minute:** My understood goal was to fill jobs and cover rent while deciding what my word was worth. The immediate task and deadlines were clear. Worker names, tabs and labelled action boxes were tappable. Roles, working hours and reference availability explained several limits. I could not state the formal end-of-month success rule; I did not reach one. I knew the next action during most scenes, but City hid the answer-phone control when a caller was waiting.

My unchecked “discreet” promise produced immediate cash. I did not observe a later consequence specifically attributable to that promise. The working-hours dispute happened, but I cannot say my unchecked quality caused it.
